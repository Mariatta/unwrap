Unwrap. Extract About

In August 2026 I posted about something that had just happened in my family.

My mother-in-law takes English classes for new immigrants. Her teacher isn't a technical person. When a lesson needs a listening exercise, he emails the class a Word document — with the MP3 audio file embedded inside it.

We couldn't get the audio out. Nothing was broken. Nobody in the house runs Windows.

The file was right there, and completely out of reach.

If you're him, this is a reasonable thing to do. Word lets you drop a sound file onto a page, it appears as a small icon, and you send one attachment instead of two. On his computer, it works. He solved his problem.

The format just doesn't survive the trip. Open that document in anything other than Word on Windows and the audio is inert — present in the file, but unreachable.

This page makes it reachable.

What's actually in the document

A .docx is a ZIP archive. Unzip one from a lesson like this and the relevant parts are:

word/document.xml The text of the page.
word/media/image1.emf The icon you see where the audio sits. Not the audio.
word/embeddings/oleObject1.bin The audio — wrapped twice.

That .bin is a compound file: the container format Office used before 2007, with its own sector allocation table and directory tree. It is, in effect, a small filesystem inside your document.

Inside it, a stream named Package (or \x01Ole10Native) begins with an OLE 1.0 Packager header — a short record holding the sender's original filename, the path the file came from on their disk, and the exact byte length of what follows. After that record: the original MP3, byte for byte.

So the audio was never converted or re-encoded. It's the teacher's exact file, sitting two containers deep. Reading it out is a matter of knowing where to look.

How this page works

Everything runs in your browser. The archive is read with JSZip, the compound file is parsed here in JavaScript, and the audio comes back as a blob you can play and save.

There is no server and no upload. Nothing about your document leaves the tab — you can disconnect from the network after the page loads and it will still work. That matters when the file belongs to someone else's classroom.

When there's nothing to find

If the sender used Insert → Object → Link to file, the audio was never embedded at all: the document holds only a path to a file on their own computer. You'll see the icon and no payload. That's the point at which the honest answer is to ask them to send the MP3 as a normal attachment.

站在他的角度，这么做完全合理。Word 允许你把一个声音文件直接放进页面，它会显示成一个小图标， 于是你只需发送一个附件，而不是两个。在他的电脑上，这套办法是行得通的。他解决了自己的问题。

只是这种格式经不起传递。除了 Windows 上的 Word，用其他任何方式打开这份文档， 音频都只是一块死数据 —— 它确实在文件里，却无法取用。

这个页面就是用来把它取出来的。

文档里究竟装了什么

.docx 本质上是一个 ZIP 压缩包。把这样一份课件解压开，关键的几个部分是：

word/document.xml 页面上的正文。
word/media/image1.emf 你在音频位置看到的那个图标，并不是音频本身。
word/embeddings/oleObject1.bin 音频本体 —— 外面套了两层包装。

这个 .bin 是一个复合文档：Office 在 2007 年之前使用的容器格式， 自带扇区分配表和目录树。可以说，它是藏在你文档内部的一个微型文件系统。

在它里面，有一个名为 Package（或 \x01Ole10Native）的数据流， 开头是一段 OLE 1.0 Packager 头部 —— 这段短记录保存了发送者的原始文件名、 该文件在其磁盘上的路径，以及紧随其后的数据的精确字节长度。这段记录之后， 就是原封不动的 MP3。

也就是说，音频从未被转换或重新编码。那正是老师电脑上的那个文件，只是被埋在两层容器之下。 要把它读出来，无非是知道该往哪里找。

这个页面是怎么工作的

所有处理都在你的浏览器里完成。压缩包由 JSZip 读取，复合文档在这里用 JavaScript 直接解析， 音频以 blob 的形式返回，可以试听，也可以保存。

没有服务器，也没有上传。你的文档不会有任何内容离开这个标签页 —— 页面加载完成后，即使断网也照样能用。当文件属于别人的课堂时，这一点尤其重要。

如果什么也找不到

如果发送者用的是「插入 → 对象 → 链接到文件」，那么音频根本就没有被嵌入进来： 文档里保存的只是一条指向他自己电脑上某个文件的路径。你只会看到图标，看不到数据。 到了这一步，诚实的做法是请对方把 MP3 作为普通附件重新发一次。
